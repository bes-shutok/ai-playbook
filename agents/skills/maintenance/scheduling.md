### Step 5: scheduling

- Assemble the child prompt from the matching blueprint in `agents/skills/maintenance/prompt-templates.md`, filling that blueprint's placeholders. Dispatch slice: for an execution child the scheduled automation prompt is ONLY the content of the `<prompt for the scheduled session>` block of the execution blueprint, with `{REPO_ROOT}` and `{some_plan}` filled. The wrapper sentences around that block (the SCHEDULER voice, "Schedule at {execution_time}") describe the deciding turn's own job and are never part of the scheduled payload; `{execution_time}` is filled into the scheduling call (the delay or cron fields), not into the payload. Unblock-dispatch slice (added 2026-09-25): an unblock child's scheduled automation prompt is the content of the unblock template's own `<prompt for the parked-dependency unblock session>` block, with the deciding turn filling `{REPO_ROOT}` and deriving the plan path and blocked_on from the dispatch target exactly as the execution slice's fill sentence derives its fills; the slice's existing sentence keeps naming the execution blueprint for ordinary execution children. Authoring slice: observed dispatch practice transmits the authoring blueprint's payload paragraph (the span opening Schedule at {schedule_time} the following task: through the paragraph's end) as the authoring child's scheduled prompt, not the whole fenced body: the deciding turn fills {REPO_ROOT} (in the payload paragraph's RE-ARM DUTY and clocked-order sentence sets), {schedule_time}, and {backlog_item} and transmits that paragraph; the payload paragraph is self-contained for the duties a child session must see (re-arm, claim lifecycle, pre-work stand-down gates, ad-hoc worktree creation, context checkpoints, compaction, review, landing), and the rest of the fenced body is the body-side specification that the payload's compact sentence sets mirror; the two field lines after the block are fill-in spec and are never part of the payload. Registry-task slice: a registry-task child's scheduled prompt is the task file's body prefixed with the recognition line (`recurring task <id> lane` plus the resolved repository root), assembled per the dispatch ladder including the re-arm-first action it requires of every child payload; the other slices' wrapper-block mechanics do not apply because the body is self-contained.
- Schedule at most one child per lane per scheduler turn (one execution AND one authoring at most), never into a lane whose child is in flight (`G1e` / `G1a`). A clocked child is a one-shot firing at least 5 minutes out: the final fire time is never earlier than now plus 5 minutes, even when the quota leg defers past a reset that lands sooner (floor lowered from 30 minutes by user request; see the Revisions ledger's 2026-09-15 floor-revision entry: the deciding turn's state write precedes the fire by construction, so a tight floor is safe). A runtime dispatch primitive without a clock (idle-time dispatch) schedules no fire time and is exempt from the 5-minute floor. When both lanes dispatch in one turn, the execution child takes the clocked create and the authoring lane takes the in-session carrier, the idle-time primitive, or defers to the next turn. The execution lane dispatches through the Execution-lane carrier-selection ordering (Step 3): the clocked create stays the primary leg and every fallback leg is available only after the precheck has retired the higher legs; the idle leg records a null fire time and relies on the idle-child lane-hold and failure-cap coverage (the fire-time bookkeeping the clocked leg exists to provide is carried on the fallback legs by the entry's own outcome and completion-evidence arms), and the in-session leg consumes no primitive at all. For an idle-time dispatch `{schedule_time}` is filled with the literal `an idle-time run (no scheduled fire time)`.
- The cap's directive-bound second execution leg (added 2026-10-06, origin docs/history/backlog/2026-10-06-parallel-execution-turn-cap-and-fired-arm.md): the "at most one child per lane per scheduler turn" default stands, and a turn carrying a live execution directive may execute one additional distinct-target plan in-session beside the turn's one clocked dispatch. A live execution directive is a standing operator directive naming execution throughput or the turn's own user-directed payload text. The second leg still re-reads every guard at the decision (the lane guards `G1e`/`G1a`, the quota leg, the `G3b` final-slot effect, the pre-dispatch landing gate, the fleet cap, and target-distinctness against every armed and pending `children[]` entry), records the standard `(in-session)` children[] entry the in-session dispatch mode already writes, and stays outside the Execution-lane carrier-selection ordering because that ordering selects the carrier for the one primary dispatch when primitives are absent, a different trigger than a capacity-parallel second execution beside a working clocked leg.
- In-session dispatch mode: when D2's quota-aware primitive selection resolves the authoring lane to in-session, no automation is created and no fire time is scheduled: the deciding turn runs the authoring blueprint's task paragraph itself, with every gate still applied (done lock, fire-time checks, and the blueprint's remaining pre-work checks), and the run's outcome is the turn's authoring record. The in-session mode precedes the idle-time primitive whenever its quota conditions hold; when they do not, the lane follows the existing clocked or idle-time rules above.
- Precondition: after the quota leg fixes the final fire time, re-evaluate the lane guard (`G1e` or `G1a`), the `G3b` final-slot effect (a fresh merge-lock hold defers the lane), and the lane's pre-dispatch landing gate against that final slot before creating the automation; if any trips, resolve to D3 and record the reason (schedule nothing). The pre-dispatch landing gate applies before creating any child or beginning an in-session run of either lane: the final-slot re-evaluation also re-runs the lane's landing gate, and an outstanding unlanded run or surviving note at the final slot resolves a freshly selected lane to D3 with the reason `pending-landing (outstanding unlanded run)` (a fresh selection is re-decided next turn; a retained `pending_dispatch` target is kept per the reader's rule). Ladder precheck: after the quota leg (for a clocked dispatch) or the dispatch decision (for an idle-time dispatch), and before any ladder step, run the runtime overlay's ladder precheck for the lane; a tripped precheck resolves the lane to D3 with the overlay's `turn_error` reason and schedules nothing; the parked `pending_dispatch` entry the precheck write creates persists until the next turn's Step 1 reader dispatches it (the precheck imports only the trap rule's park-or-retain write, never its clear-on-idle-success clause) except that the execution lane first walks its Execution-lane carrier-selection ordering (Step 3): the idle leg after a tripped clocked leg, the in-session leg when both dispatch legs lack their primitives, and only a lane with no available dispatch leg parks, that park pairing with the resume carrier per the ordering.
- Record the child's automation id, kind, target, and fire time in the state file (Step 6); an idle-time dispatch records a null id and fire time with an `idle` marker on the target; an in-session dispatch records a null id and fire time with an `(in-session)` marker on the target, mirroring the `(idle)` marker convention. An execution dispatch also records `dispatch_plan_sha` (the target plan file's sha256 at dispatch time) so the failure cap's stand-down carve-out can compare the plan file against its dispatch-time state.
