Any active churn or recurrence trigger means non-convergence: run review-reconciliation before another panel, then invoke `self-heal` to assess and carry out an authorized simplification or recovery before any required pause. Follow that policy's attempt allowance and stop conditions. Without an active trigger, the shared policy determines convergence and continuation. Record the previous and current blocker counts and the controlling trigger in `extensions.convergence` for each round. A configured cap alone does not trigger reconciliation.

The trajectory record preserves the counts used for each continuation decision.
14. **Fold receipts:** After every fold batch, run a mechanical audit that each accepted finding's fix actually landed (the pinned span occurs in the new bytes, or the prescribed behavior exists) before the batch is marked triaged; a claimed-but-unlanded fold is an orchestrator error to record in the staging doc and re-execute, never a silent pass. Every fold batch that edits the plan artifact runs the fold-gate-dispatch ordering (review-plan Step 5 rule 12) on the folded bytes, per that rule's trigger, dispatch semantics, and outcome-line grammar; the ordering never holds dispatch on a proceeded or degraded outcome.
15. **Case-growth cap:** When the plan's prescribed test list grows by more than 50 percent across rounds relative to its pre-loop count, answer the simplification question (are these cases testing the design or testing the pins?) before the next fold or round and record the answer in the staging doc.
16. At every configured round cap, perform the trajectory reflection in rule 12. Cap alone does not stop reviews or trigger reconciliation. Apply the convergence and active-trigger rules in rule 13, and preserve any configured cap-closure terminal mechanics under their existing contract. A cap-shaped stop or park routes through the Uncertain workflow stops duty before the stop is judged unrecoverable.
17. **Full-panel budget beside the cap:** the full-panel budget is 2 full-panel rounds for a declared execution budget of 2 hours or less, plus 1 full-panel round per additional started 2 hours (a 3-4 hour plan gets 3, a 5-6 hour plan gets 4); a declaration beyond 6 hours never buys a larger budget (the ceiling is D-8 clause (3)'s policy, not a plan-sizing threshold), and such a plan remains the plan-sizing doctrine's split business at the task level. A plan carrying no declared budget line keeps the configured interval semantics; delta rounds never consume the budget; breaching it pauses the loop for operator direction per review-plan's Scope and complexity policy duty (extend the derived budget by a stated number of full-panel rounds, continue for one further full-panel round, or park and report; an unattended lane parks and reports), the park recorded only after one self-heal consultation for a non-user-only blocker per the maintenance decisions' non-user-only blocker line before another full-panel round launches.

**Ready for execution** means the latest review artifact explicitly states `ready=yes` with zero unresolved blocking findings, except through the cap-closure terminal shape; any open material grill question or unresolved scope-extension decision is not ready, whatever the review verdict says. Open the artifact and verify its verdict rather than relying on chat summary. The post-fold digest requirement (rule 6) is enforced by the `--source-plan` mechanical gate in `review-plan` Step 4, which accepts the two exit paths rule 6 defines; a `ready=yes` recorded before a fold fails that gate and does not count.

**Drafting mode:** Conversational Plan Mode is a DRAFTING mode, not a completed plan lifecycle. When the conversation cannot save, review, and finalize the plan artifact, say that review and artifact finalization are pending.

Then verify these structural failure modes and fix them in the plan:

- **Current ownership:** if a prior phase extracted or renamed the owner of behavior, put new work in the final owner, not the old location. Avoid "implement in A, then move to B" churn unless the refactor itself is the goal.
- **Coherent commits:** each task ending in a commit must leave the code compiling. Do not split one required model/signature propagation across multiple commits when the intermediate state cannot compile.
- **Constructor-signature-change audit:** when a task adds, removes, or reorders a constructor parameter on an existing class, grep the whole repo for `new ClassName(` before finalizing the task and list every direct construction site explicitly (production and test). A compatibility method overload preserves *behavior* for existing callers but does not preserve *construction* call sites, which still need the new argument to compile. For each listed site, state whether it exercises the changed method (needs a configured stub proving the new behavior) or not (an unconfigured mock is enough); do not describe a test as "unaffected" without checking whether it stubs/verifies the method the constructor change feeds into.
- **Right-layer tests:** place failing tests at the layer that can observe the behavior. A mocked downstream collaborator cannot verify logic owned by that collaborator.
- **Side-effect safety:** when adding a guard around an irreversible side effect, specify failure semantics explicitly (claim/confirm/release, fail-open/fail-closed, TTL) so retries do not skip work that never succeeded.
- **Existing constants and config:** verify whether metrics, properties, flags, or key prefixes already exist before planning new ones. Reuse existing names unless the RFC requires a new external contract.
- **Validation minimality:** avoid redundant validation commands. Prefer the narrowest command that proves the task, and a final scoped `verify` when it subsumes compile/test; but never narrower than the two-tier Review Scope (see **Validation Commands (authoring rules)**).
- **Review scope completeness:** explicit must-fix covers all task `Files:`; plan-related extension policy is stated; validation breadth matches contract changes the plan introduces.
- **Claim-to-task ownership sweep (the reverse direction):** every behavior or design claim in `## Gist & Examples`, `## Design Invariants (CR Guard)`, `## Evaluation Criteria`, and the Assumptions decision receipts traces to an owning task checkbox; before the first review round, sweep those four claim sections against the task list. The same pre-review boundary carries the cross-task trace sweep beside the claims sweep, four named trace dimensions: the outcome trace, where each user-visible outcome names its producer path, its emission point before the consuming decision (the word none when there is no timing dependence), its implementing file and section, and a scoped verification pin; the Ship-when trace, where a Ship-when condition the author classifies as a release gate or operations follow-up names its accepted class (EXTERNAL_RELEASE_GATE or OPERATIONS_FOLLOW_UP), its evidence owner, a measurable closure condition, and evidence sources that each trace to an explicit task output, while a routine completion condition states none for the class and keeps the evidence-owner and closure checks; the validation trace, where every scoped changed file (a file listed in a task's `Files:` block or the Review Scope must-fix list) appears in the validation battery, with an explicit em-dash scan and a public-hygiene scan (files mode, repository root set) for each scoped non-Markdown path; the shared-state trace, where the fold contract-term rule above (a fold that changes a contract term greps the whole plan for the superseded term and re-derives every matching bullet) is applied at this sweep boundary to every shared lifecycle state term across all task sections, per task phase for a legitimate multi-state cutover, so a correction to one task cannot leave another task describing a different state. The review-side closure matrix runs task-to-artifact; a claim with no owning task survives every per-task gate silently and surfaces only as a mid-loop blocking finding (witnessed at r1).
- **Language-specific testing traps:** before finalizing test tasks, link to the language guidelines for this project (e.g. `kotlin_guidelines.md`, `python_guidelines.md`) in the plan header so the implementer has the relevant silent-failure patterns at hand. For metrics coverage, also link to the applicable company or project guidelines.
- **Branch count verification:** when specifying helper extraction from a branching function, count all conditional branches in the function body before writing the task. An incomplete branch list silently omits emission paths.
- **Symbol-move patch audit:** when a task moves symbols between modules, grep the whole test tree for EVERY patch idiom on the moved names: `monkeypatch.setattr(<module>, "<name>", ...)` AND string-form `unittest.mock.patch("<module>.<name>")`. List each site in the task and require same-commit retargeting to the new owning module; un-retargeted string-form patches fail loudly at import, while un-retargeted `setattr` patches fail SILENTLY (the attribute still exists as a leftover or the patch simply stops intercepting), letting real loaders run inside a characterization net.
- **Recovery-claim feasibility:** when a task specifies a fallback, partial re-scan, or partial-recovery mechanism, trace the new fixture's given/expects through the SPECIFIED mechanism against the real code before finalizing; a fixture expectation the mechanism cannot deliver (for example recovery of content lying inside the malformed region a reset re-scan provably skips) is a spec defect the implementer can only satisfy by improvising a different mechanism. State what the mechanism does NOT recover when today's behavior already loses it, and place a straddling item's load-bearing state before the fallback boundary. (Witness: a plan review caught a seeding contract promising recovery of post-boundary bullets the heading-reset re-scan skips; only reviewer simulation of the real parser state machine exposed it.)
- **Shared-seam overlap:** given a plan whose `Files:` paths overlap a sibling open plan's `Files:` paths on the same file section, the authoring session adjudicates the seam before finalizing and records the disposition in `## Assumptions` naming the sibling plan. The three dispositions: re-anchor on the sibling's landed form when the sibling already executed; declare the header execution dependency under the Seam rule when both plans are open; drop the duplicated span as already covered when the sibling's landed form already delivers the edit. The posture is advisory: the adjudication adds no gate, validator, or refusal; an unadjudicated seam is a review finding, never a mechanical stop.

**Pre-finalization self-check (companion to the readiness definition above):** before describing any plan as ready for execution, answer these six questions:

1. Was `review-plan` invoked for this plan?
2. Does the latest Markdown review and `.stats.json` sidecar exist?
3. Does the sidecar digest match the final plan bytes, or does a verified corrections chain carry the delta with its terminal digest matching the final plan bytes?
4. Does the latest review report `ready=yes` (except through the cap-closure terminal shape)?
5. Are there zero unresolved blocking findings?
6. Does any material grill question or scope-extension decision remain open (any `open:` receipt in the decision-points trailer)?

**Loud-finish gate (mechanical):** before describing the plan as ready for execution or delivered, run the readiness validator's full-gate mode over the plan (python3 scripts/plan_readiness.py <plan-path>). A failure in the no-review-artifact or missing-sidecar class is reported by name in the authoring session's final report, quoting the validator's reason line; the session does not describe the plan as delivered while that failure stands. The drafting-mode caveat is unchanged: a conversational draft states review finalization is pending instead of claiming delivery.

Do not describe a plan as ready when any answer is negative, except through the cap-closure terminal shape or the graded digest-match positive above. `scripts/plan_readiness.py` is the fail-closed mechanical counterpart called by `execute-plan` and `done`; a plan only in conversation is negative by construction. It uses the four-outcome vocabulary in `scripts/OUTCOME_CONTRACT.md`: only exit 0 (`OUTCOME: pass`) or exit 1 (`OUTCOME: fail` naming every failed condition) is a readiness verdict. Exit 2/3 or no final `OUTCOME:` line is indeterminate or tool error, never a verdict. The authoring `--pre-round` gate (rule 29) reports decision-trailer, Review Scope, plan-ownership, classification-tag, Primary-class-anchors-grammar, and extraction-rule-executability defects before review starts. The authoring-time preflight is owned by rule 29 in `agents/skills/plans/validation-authoring-rules.md`: run every pinned validation command in the plan's own Validation Commands block once over the plan bytes before the first review round, and record the outcomes in the Validation preamble. A green pass leaves the full gate to re-prove review-record bindings. A same-slug `readiness PRE-ROUND WARNING` is advisory; source-plan identity keeps review series separate.
